## Read this first

This is a faithful picture of the library as it stands, including its defects. It was built read-only: nothing in the live library was altered, merged or deleted to make it. Where the material has a known rough edge, the edge is disclosed on the volume itself rather than smoothed over.

### The dictionary (Volumes 1–4)

Each volume was re-read directly from its typeset PDF using the page typography (bold headword, roman definition, italic source line), which pairs every definition with the right headword exactly. Where the same term appears in more than one edition, the editions now sit under one heading, each labeled. About 4,750 headwords whose letters had been split by the original scan (*A DM I N I C ULAR EVI D ENCE*) were closed back up — only where the closed form matched a clean copy elsewhere in the volume or resolved to real words; letters were never changed. Definition text is verbatim throughout, so character-level scan damage inside definitions remains and is not marked.

**One entry to treat with care.** Under *ASSAULT* in Volume 1, the Black's Law text is not an assault definition — the library record holds the text of the neighbouring entry *adscriptus*, run in during the original import. The Cornell Wex definition under the same heading is correct. About 211 records in A–C show this run-together pattern; each is listed in the volume's grouping log.

### Statutes (Volumes 7–8)

The Missouri set covers RSMo chapter 1 and chapter 400 only, plus the constitution, the CSR and municipal codes. The other chapters — including 455, 562, 563, 565 and 575 — were not reachable when the export ran: the live library stores some chapter-1 sections up to 257 times over, and the export's 10,000-record ceiling was spent on those duplicates. The five Marion County case statutes are supplied at the top of Volume 7 as excerpts verified against revisor.mo.gov. The federal set (8,844 CFR and U.S.C. sections) is split into three parts to keep each page fast.

### Case summaries (Volumes 9–10)

15,021 case records. Two thirds carry no stored summary — the citation, court and year are still there, and each of those entries is marked *citation record only* so it cannot be mistaken for a real holding. 5,356 entries carry a genuine summary and holding.

### Encyclopedia and Latin (Volumes 5–6)

Both were read to exhaustion from the PDF. Encyclopedia paragraph breaks could not be recovered from the print, so some article bodies run together; the text itself is intact. The Latin volume is complete at 219 terms with no damage found.

### Duplication

Every volume was checked entry by entry after the rebuild: no volume lists the same headword or the same statute citation twice, no dictionary term appears in two dictionary volumes, and no entry repeats a passage of its own text. Where one dictionary term carries the same wording under two editions, the wording is shown once with both edition labels. Where a statute is stored more than once with different text, the fuller text is shown and the other stored copy is tucked under it. Sixty-five case records were merged because they were the same opinion under two captions. What remains and is *not* duplication: a dictionary definition and an encyclopedia article on the same term are different works and both are kept; and unrelated cases that share a citation are Missouri and federal table decisions reported on the same page, so they stay separate.
